# W10: kinds of island

Features: W10, W01, W06, W04, W09, A04, S03

Islands now come in five kinds (beach, forest, mountain, snowy, desert)
at different heights, with trees, rocks and the like, and the bridges
climb between them. NOTES.md → W10 has how it works; this entry is the
why and what happened on the way.

## Decided (user's, 2026-10-08)

Asked as choices with a recommendation each; the user gave no reasons
beyond the choice.

- **Biomes:** all four offered besides the beach: forest, mountain,
  snowy and desert (I had recommended forest and mountain only).
- **Picker:** chosen per island in the sessions menu (rejected: one
  biome per cluster from the repository's name; the same with some
  mixing; random per island with no choice). A follow-up settled the
  details: per island, not per cluster; the default is Random (rejected:
  from the repository's name; the beach); nothing is remembered, like
  clusters.
- **Heights:** bridges land at each island's own height, with steps
  where steep (rejected: every island keeping a low beach for bridges;
  steeper ramps only).
- **Props:** made in code, placeholders (rejected: Kenney's Nature Kit).

The user then went to bed and asked me to finish W10 on my own, taking
the smaller decisions myself and listing them for later. Those are
below.

## My picks, for the user to confirm

- Heights: edge and middle per biome (NOTES → W10), mountains with
  hills of up to 16 m over the middle's rise (the noise seldom reaches
  its top: peaks measured 14-16 m above the water) and snow above
  12.5 m.
- Every non-beach island has a narrow beach (so you can always swim
  ashore, W08's rule), a slope up to a **shoulder** at its edge height
  where bridges land, and three **hill-free valleys** to the middle.
- Prop kinds per biome, their densities, and that bushes and shrubs
  aren't solid.
- The menu row is called "Biome:" and sits under "Cluster:".
- Sessions already running when the game starts get a random biome,
  so they change kind on every launch.
- The minimap and big map colour islands by biome.
- Steps are only drawn: the collision stays a ramp, so walking up them
  is smooth.

## What was built

- `Biome` holds each kind's profile; `IslandGenerator` takes a kind.
  The beach keeps W01's exact shape (a "legacy" flag), so nothing about
  existing islands changed.
- `IslandProps` makes each prop's mesh once from Godot's primitives
  (made flat-shaded by computing one normal per triangle; crowns and
  rocks jittered with the same offset for vertices in the same place, so
  they stay closed) and draws one MultiMesh per kind per island.
- Bridges needed no change to land at different heights: W06 built
  them for any slope. Between islands they run about 50 m, so they rarely
  get steep; the steps show mostly on short ones.

## Tuning from screenshots

- The first mountains looked like hills: the hill noise was clipped to
  0-1 after a gain of 2.5, giving flat tops at 9 m. A lower gain (1.5)
  and 16 m make pointed peaks.
- Peak snow lost to the rock colour on steep tops; snow is checked
  first now.
- Forest, snowy and desert looked right at the first try.

## Walkability

Peaks could wall in an island's middle. Rather than hope, three
valleys, 4 m wide at a random turn, keep hills off the way up, and the
world test walks a 1 m grid (no step over 40°) from the beach and every
landing to the middle for each biome over five seeds. On small islands
the slope used to shrink with everything else and got too steep; now the
beach and the shoulder shrink first.

## Performance

Measured as W09 (interleaved A/B pairs, median of 5) by hiding all
props on the demo's 64 islands of random biomes: 0.25 ms looking out
from the platform, 0.5 ms looking back, 1.1 ms standing in a forest.
Small enough to leave props on the islands' draw distance with no range
of their own.

## Bugs on the way

- A test that built a `Bridge` inside `_initialize` saw no meshes:
  `_ready` doesn't run until the tree is going. The check awaits a frame.
- Waiting for a sunk island with a lambda that captured it logged
  "Lambda capture at index 0 was freed" once it was gone; the wait
  captures its instance id instead.
