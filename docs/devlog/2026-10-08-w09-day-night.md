# W09: day, night and the weather

Features: W09, W02, W08, W01, A01

The sun and the moon now go round a 28-minute day, the weather drifts
between clear, cloudy, rain, wind and storms, and each island lights up
at night. NOTES.md → W09 has how it works; this entry is the why and
what happened on the way.

## Decided (user's, 2026-10-08)

The user's choices; they gave no reasons beyond the choice.

- **Day:** 28 minutes, half day and half night, sun down and moon up;
  every launch starts at mid-morning.
- **Weather:** random and gentle and meaning nothing, so it can't be
  read as an agent's state (A03, A05): mostly clear, drifting now and
  then into clouds, rain, wind on the waves (W02) or a storm.
- **Thunderstorms:** rain, wind and soft, rare lightning (a gentle
  brightening, never a white-out, at most one every 10-20 s), with a
  bolt in the distance; an off switch in the settings menu later.
- **Night:** moonlit and readable, with stars and warm lights on each
  island: torches (W01) and a lamp on the orchestrator's desk (A01).
- **Sound:** none yet; hooks (`Weather.lightning`) for a later thunder
  and rain sound.

Colours, energies, counts and timings were left to me.

## Tuning from screenshots

Screenshots in both renderers at set hours and weathers (the dev flags
`--hour`, `--weather`, `--day-minutes`), then:

- **Storms** looked like plain overcast: a darker storm sky
  (`STORM_SKY`) and 40 bigger clouds.
- **Night sand looked snowy.** That was the moon's direct light, not the
  ambient: `MOON_ENERGY` down to 0.12, `NIGHT_AMBIENT_ENERGY` 0.35, so
  night stays readable mostly through the ambient colour.
- **Under the water at night** the sea floor glowed against the dark
  fog (W08): light and ambient drop to 40 % there.
- **Sunsets** were washed out over the whole sky: warmer dusk colours,
  and the sky's gradient eased with `1 - exp(-6 * up)` so they stay a
  band near the horizon.
- **Lightning** barely showed: the sky lifts by 0.7 of the flash, and
  the bolt is wider (4 m at the top, about 2 m at the sea). The bolt had been drawn all along; the earlier
  shots had just missed its moment.

**How the screenshots were taken:** a throwaway SceneTree script whose
`_initialize` awaits one frame before touching any autoload, with a
watchdog that quits after a while. Without the watchdog, an early
script error leaves a grey window that never closes.

## Performance

Measured with a throwaway script on the layout demo (1920x1080, vsync
off), alternating the two versions in pairs and taking the median of 5,
since most of these changes are smaller than the 1-3 ms runs vary by.
Numbers are in NOTES.md → Architecture → Performance.

- **The sky cost 1.6-1.9 ms a frame** because `DayNight` set its shader
  uniforms every frame, and each set redraws the sky's reflection map.
  `_set_sky` now sets a uniform only when it moved by at least
  `SKY_STEP` (1/255; the sun's direction, the fastest, steps about once
  a second); the
  sky now measures about 0 ms. The world test checks a tiny change
  isn't sent.
- **Rain rings** (`ocean.gdshader`) are drawn only within 60 m.
- **Tried and reverted:** drawing only the side of the ocean you can
  see (a `cull_back` / `cull_front` split). It measured -0.6 ms on
  one view and +0.7 ms on another, so no gain worth the extra code.
- The rest: clouds 0.2-1.6 ms by view, night lights 0-1.6 ms near an
  island, rain about 0; the ocean (about 3 ms) and the sea floor
  (0.6-1.3 ms) are W08's, unchanged. The platform view: 25.4 ms of GPU
  against 24.4 ms on `main`, within the spread; the user's Godot editor
  was open at the time, sharing the integrated GPU.

## Bugs on the way

- A Python edit on Windows wrote `day_night.gd` as cp1252, which broke
  it at a degree sign in a comment. Files are now always opened with
  `io.open(..., encoding='utf-8', newline='')`.
