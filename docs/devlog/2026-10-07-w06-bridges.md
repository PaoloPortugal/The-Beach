# W06: bridges, wedges and the minimap

Features: W06, W05, W04, W07

Bridges join each cluster's islands and each cluster to the spawn
platform, the clusters moved from rings 360 m out to wedges fanning out
from the platform, and a minimap shows the cluster you're in.

## Decisions (user's, 2026-10-07)

Recorded in NOTES → W06 before building; the first set (a tree, bridges
made in code, appearing and vanishing for now, rails, a minimap now, a
bridge from the platform, an empty island's bridges counting as "on it")
came in an earlier session. In this one:

- **Re-linking a cut tree:** patch the gap. Every bridge that still
  stands stays, and only the shortest new bridges joining the pieces
  appear, so nothing moves under you. Rejected: rebuilding the whole tree
  each time (bridges you know could move); leaving an empty hub where the
  island was (a new object in the world, and art).
- **The platform's bridge:** to the cluster's nearest island. The user
  pointed out that the middle island and the nearest are the same in
  practice: the first island is the only one when the bridge is made, and
  it re-links when that island sinks either way. So the platform is just
  part of each cluster's tree.
- **Layout:** I recommended keeping it (360 m is the smallest ring that
  kept two 7-island clusters apart). The user wanted clusters closer to
  the platform and proposed **cones**: wedges that widen outward, set in
  a ring around the platform, noting that it raises how many clusters fit.
  I put two readings to them (8 wedges with an outer ring for overflow;
  big and small wedges interleaved) and they picked the first.
- **Overflow:** after a packing check my first numbers were wrong. A
  45° wedge holds 8 islands within about 450 m (not 10), and only 8 of 16
  outer wedges could have a straight bridge from the platform (the other
  8 would cross an inner cluster). The user chose **16 clusters with
  straight bridges**: 8 inner wedges of up to 10 islands, 8 outer ones on
  the corridors between them; past that an island goes to a far spot,
  teleport only. Rejected: 24 with bridges that bend at junction decks;
  8 inner wedges only.
- **Minimap:** the cluster you're in or nearest, top left (A05's count
  is top right), north-up, M toggles, as I recommended. Rejected: the
  whole world (islands become dots), a radar turning with you.
- **A demo** with 64 fake sessions in many clusters, to see how it all
  looks (the user's request, mid-build).

## Built

- *Wedge spots:* a greedy scan, outward in 2 m rows, then sideways from
  the middle, taking the first point 100 m from earlier spots and 50 m
  inside the wedge's edges. The 50 m margin keeps an island's land
  (at most about 44 m: radius 30 m, noise up to +45 %) off the corridor
  lines that outer clusters' platform bridges run along, and makes islands
  in neighbouring wedges 100 m apart. The first island lands 133 m out.
  Outer wedges start at 650 m: at 560 m an outer island came 65 m from an
  inner one (a Python check before the GDScript).
- *Bridges:* `Bridge` is one mesh and one concave collision shape per
  bridge, like the islands. The deck arches to 1.2 m over the water so the
  waves (±0.35 m) stay under it. The rails' collision is a 2.2 m wall,
  higher than a jump (1.5 m), since "rails you can't walk through" is for
  not falling in the sea; the visible rail is 1 m.
- *Tree:* `BridgeNetwork` knows nothing of islands: regions with a
  centre, a reach and an edge Callable, so W10's other biomes and heights
  plug in. A new bridge prefers a line clear of other regions' land; that
  matters for patches, which could otherwise run a bridge across a third
  island.
- *Minimap:* first fitted every island of the cluster, which zoomed right
  out when a cluster had islands in far spots; now it fits the islands in
  its wedge, or just the far one you're near.

## Bugs found while building

- The deck's clearance was measured from the bridge's start instead of
  the water, so decks sat 0.5 m too high above their ends. The walking
  test caught it (standing at 1.70 m on a 1.20 m deck).
- Checks after `_frames` (physics frames) can read state `_process`
  hasn't updated yet when a heavy scene runs several physics steps per
  frame; the minimap checks wait for process frames (`_settle`), as the
  world test already does.
