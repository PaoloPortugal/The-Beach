# W08: swimming and the seabed

Features: W08, W01, W02, W03, W04, W06, W07

## Decided (user's, 2026-10-08)

Asked as choices with a recommendation each; the user gave no reasons
beyond the choice.

- **Diving:** dive freely with no breath limit (rejected: a breath meter;
  surface swimming only).
- **Water:** clear in the shallows, deepening to blue (rejected: evenly
  translucent everywhere; opaque except the beach shallows).
- **Sea floor:** one seabed everywhere (rejected: a seabed near islands
  only, fading into fog below).
- **Getting out:** only by walking up a beach (rejected: my
  recommendation, climbing out at any edge; ladders).
- **Way back**, since the sea respawn goes and the platform has no
  beach: a "Platform" entry in the sessions menu (rejected: a sandbar by
  the platform; both). Then, unprompted: clicking the platform on the big
  map does the same, like clicking an island.
- **Islands and the seabed:** islands carry their shelf and drop-off and
  rise out of the seabed and sink fully back in (rejected: a fixed shelf
  that stays when the island sinks into it).
- **Controls:** swim where you look, Space up, Ctrl down (rejected: level
  swimming with keys for depth).
- **Crouch and slide** (asked mid-build: here if small, else after
  W08-W10): my call was a feature of its own, W11, after W10. It's feel
  work on W03's movement (a shorter capsule, standing back up under low
  ceilings, a slide's speed and length) that would make this branch
  harder to review. Ctrl, which sinks you in the water, can crouch on land.

## Spike: see-through water in both renderers

A throwaway scene (one island, a deep floor, the shader reading
`hint_depth_texture`) screenshotted under Forward+ and Compatibility. Both
give the depth buffer to a transparent material; Compatibility's depth
has to be mapped from [-1, 1] (`CURRENT_RENDERER`). The first try looked
wrong, deep water reading shallow; a debug view of the computed depth
showed the maths was right and the "deep" area was the island's own flat
-4 m seabed, a 136 m square under the island. That square is now a shelf
and a drop-off. An all-white island under Compatibility was the known
shadow wash-out (world.gd turns the sun's shadows off there), not the
water.

## Built

- `Seabed`: a height field the same every run, in 64 m chunks around the
  camera with collision, shallow by the platform, banks, deeper water and
  trenches (-14 to -30 m).
- Islands: shelf at -4 m, drop-off to -36 m; the coarse 8 m blocks now
  start below -5 m, so the visible shelf keeps its 2 m grid. W04's
  `SUNK_Y` is 4 m under the deepest floor (was -8 m, chosen for opaque
  water). Bridge posts reach into the floor under each post; the platform
  is a block down to its floor.
- Ocean shader: tint by vertical depth, opacity by distance through the
  water, an underside; `Underwater` swaps the fog while the camera is
  under.
- Player: swimming, floating at the surface, no climbing out.

## Bugs on the way

- **Stuck at the beach.** Swimming into the beach slid the player
  sideways along the bumpy slope at the waterline: the water's easing
  took back the upward push the slope gave each frame, and only standing
  on floor-angled ground ended swimming. Now swimming into ground that
  slopes up rides up it, and touching it in shallow water ends swimming;
  a small hop on leaving the water is cleared too.

## Checked

- World test: the seabed built around you, the floor's depths, swimming
  not sinking, the underwater fog and back, floating at the surface and
  no higher, Ctrl, diving where you look, the floor stopping you, swimming
  to an island's beach and walking out, the menu's "Platform" and the big
  map's platform click. Island tests now check the shelf and the
  drop-off below the deepest floor; the performance test the coarse
  drop-off against the larger grid. All Godot suites pass.
- Screenshots, both renderers: from the platform, above an island's
  shelf, high up, under the water toward a drop-off and looking up.
