# The performance pass: far bridges, a coarse seabed, nothing past the fog

Features: W01, W06, C02 (NOTES → Architecture → Performance)

The user asked for optimizations, "perhaps culling what isn't visible",
after the W06 layout demo (64 sessions, 20 clusters, 56 bridges) ran
slowly here.

## Measuring first (2026-10-07)

A throwaway script loaded the layout demo in a 1920x1080 window with
vsync off, froze the player at fixed viewpoints and read the viewport's
GPU time, triangles and draw calls over 180 frames, each change measured
between two fresh baselines. The machine has an integrated AMD Radeon,
and other Godot windows were sharing it, so the absolute numbers are
pessimistic; the differences are what count.

The game is GPU-bound: 28-30 ms of GPU a frame, 1.5 million triangles,
more than half of them in the sun's shadow pass (4 splits, 120 m). CPU
rendering was about 1 ms, physics 0.3-0.5 ms, about 250 draw calls.
Savings per frame from hiding one thing at a time:

| What | GPU saved |
|---|---|
| Bridges hidden (345k triangles; each plank a 12-triangle box every 0.5 m) | 8.5-11 ms |
| Sun shadows off | 10 ms |
| Seabed trimmed (78 % of each island's 9,248 triangles are under the opaque water, still drawn with the depth pre-pass off) | 6.5 ms |
| Ocean hidden | 4-7 ms |
| Islands not casting shadows | 3-4.5 ms |
| 2 shadow splits instead of 4 | 0-3 ms |
| Islands and bridges past the 390 m fog hidden | 1-2.5 ms (40 of 64 islands, 136 m wide with their seabed, reach into view from the spawn) |
| Half resolution | 12 ms |
| Bubbles | ~0 empty, +5 ms with all 64 showing text (each redrew every frame) |
| Collision off, per-frame scripts off | nothing measurable |

Making an island stalled 140-170 ms (64 ms of it the generator, mostly
the seabed); loading 64 sessions took 9-11 s.

## Decisions (user's, 2026-10-07)

All core scope. Build four:
1. **A far version for bridges**, in ~24 m segments so a long bridge's
   near part isn't judged by its middle, each with its up-close look and
   a simple stand-in past ~70 m. The user's rule: the bridges are a
   placeholder, so build only what also works for bridges made from 3D
   models later. The segments stay; trimming unseen plank faces
   (placeholder-only) was not wanted. Collision unchanged.
2. **A coarse seabed** on an ~8 m grid, kept (not trimmed away), with no
   cracks; collision uses the same faces.
3. **Hide what the fog hides fully**: islands, their agents and bridges,
   at the fog's end plus their own size, read from the one fog setting.
   The demo's view from above (fog off) turns it off.
4. **Bubbles redraw only on a change** and hide past ~60 m.

Not chosen: islands stop casting shadows (the user: keep shadows as they
are), 2 shadow splits, a resolution setting (later, in a graphics
settings menu), threaded island generation, merging meshes, disabling
far collision.

## Built

- **Coarse seabed** (`IslandGenerator.build`). The grid's cells (68 x 68)
  are grouped in 4 x 4 blocks; a block whose 25 vertices are all below
  -1 m becomes two triangles over its corners. For each coarse block's
  side next to a fine block, the three fine vertices on that side are
  moved onto the line between its corners (a lerp, height included), so
  the fine triangles meet the coarse edge exactly: a T-junction with no
  crack. The jitter and the land are untouched. 3,000-3,400 triangles an
  island instead of 9,248, and the generator takes 25 ms instead of 54 (timed side by side, 20
  seeds; the first measurement's 64 ms included more around it).
  The grid is now centred (it ran 67.2 m one way and 68.8 m the other).
- **Bridge segments** (`Bridge`). The deck's run is cut evenly into
  segments of at most 24 m. Planks, posts and rails go into the segment
  where they start, as before (same random sequence), into a `Near`
  mesh with `visibility_range_end` 70 m; a `Far` mesh with
  `visibility_range_begin` 70 m has the deck as one slab (top and two
  sides, in 4 m pieces), one top rail a side and four-sided posts down
  to just under the water: about a fifth of the triangles. Both have a
  4 m margin. Godot measures from the box's middle and, with fading off,
  uses the margins as hysteresis: an instance that is showing hides only
  past the end plus the margin, one that is hidden shows only inside the
  end minus it. With the same numbers on both, near and far swap on the
  same frame, at 74 m going away and 66 m coming back.
- **Draw distance** (`DrawDistance`, `SessionIslands.draw_distance`).
  `SessionIslands` listens to `SceneTree.node_added`; any
  `GeometryInstance3D` under it gets `visibility_range_end` = distance +
  half its box's diagonal, deferred so its mesh is set. That covers the
  islands, orchestrators, markers, far bridges and subagents that pop in
  later, without each of them knowing. Geometry that already has a range
  (near bridges, bubbles) keeps it; A05's beacons are meant to be seen
  through the fog and carry `DrawDistance.UNLIMITED`. Setting the
  distance reapplies it to everything in the `draw_distance` group.
  `world.gd` → `fit_draw_distance` sets it from the WorldEnvironment's
  `fog_depth_end`, or 0 with the fog off; the demo's B calls it after
  toggling the fog.
- **Bubbles** (`MessageBubble`). The `SubViewport` was `UPDATE_ALWAYS`.
  Now `show_text` sets `UPDATE_ONCE` after laying out (Godot flushes the
  deferred container sorting before it draws, so the drawing is of the
  finished layout) and `UPDATE_DISABLED` when it hides; the texture keeps
  the last drawing; the same text again (the chat updates the bubble on
  any message in the session) draws nothing. The sprite has `visibility_range_end` 60 m.

## Results (2026-10-08)

Back to back, the old branch and this one, twice each, same script and
viewpoints:

| View | Before: GPU, triangles | After: GPU, triangles |
|---|---|---|
| Platform, looking along +x | 25.4-26.3 ms, 1.42 M | 17.9-18.0 ms, 0.42 M |
| Platform, the other way | 27.2-27.9 ms, 1.76 M | 17.6-17.9 ms, 0.45 M |
| On island d01 | 23.3 ms, 1.17 M | 15.8-16.5 ms, 0.33 M |
| Middle of a bridge | 25.0-29.4 ms, 1.55-1.61 M | 15.4-16.2 ms, 0.42 M |

About a third less GPU time per frame, and
a quarter of the triangles. Draw calls went up from about 240 to
210-330 and CPU rendering from 0.8 to 1.2 ms: two meshes per bridge
segment instead of one per bridge. The earlier estimate of ~12 ms
was optimistic: hiding parts again showed that the bridges' near
versions still cost 4-5 ms around the platform, where 16 bridges start
and their first segments are all within 70 m (their far versions cost
about 0.3 ms); the sun's shadows about 4.5 ms; the islands 2-9 ms by
view. The near distance or the placeholder planks are where more would
come from, if needed.

Checked in a window under both renderers: the near and far versions
match at the seam, and the bubble's texture didn't change when its label
was changed behind its back, then did after `show_text`. Headless Godot
draws nothing, so the new suite (`tests/world/performance_test.gd`)
checks how each piece is set up.

## Bugs and dead ends

- The first crack check (every vertex lies on whatever triangle is under
  it) failed on land: the 2 m grid's jitter, with alternating diagonals,
  occasionally folds a vertex just inside a neighbouring triangle. That
  is old and invisible from above; the check now looks only at vertices
  on coarse triangles' edges, which is what can crack.
- The draw distance first used the global scale for the size. A subagent
  pops in by scaling up from 0, so its range depended on the frame it
  was set; it uses the unscaled box now.
