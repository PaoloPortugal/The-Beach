# D01: fishing

Features: D01, C15, D03, W01, W06, W08

D01 built on branch `d01-fishing`: the rod in hand (C15's slot 2)
casts, a fish comes, nibbles and bites, and a click on the bite catches
it. The design is NOTES → D01 (dev log 2026-10-27-d01-fishing-design);
the look is a placeholder.

## The user's decisions (2026-10-27, before the build)

- **No catch-bar minigame for now.** The user doesn't want fishing to
  be too unoriginal, and the catch bars they know are Stardew Valley's
  and Webfishing's. So a fish is caught by **hooking it on the real
  bite**: it comes, nibbles, then bites, and a click in the bite's
  window catches it.
- **A fight of our own may come later.** A candidate is in-world line
  tension (the rod bends and the line strains, read from the world
  rather than from a bar). Open.
- **A05's pulse during a fight is moot** until a fight returns; decide
  it again then.
- **Casting:** hold left click to charge, release to cast where you
  look. Left click while waiting reels in, and reeling in loses nothing.
- **Three placeholder species**, one per water, until the user supplies
  the real list.
- **Catches are recorded now** for D03 (the collection), in `user://`.

## My picks

- **Reach** 4-22 m from the player along the look direction flattened
  onto the water, and a ray straight down there for land: under 0.3 m
  deep counts as land (an island, a deck, the platform), and there's
  no cast.
- **Water kind by depth** where the bobber lands: under 4.5 m the
  shallows (the shelf is 4 m down), under 12 m the drop-off, else the
  open sea.
- **Timings:** a 4-12 s wait, 1-3 nibbles, a 0.9 s bite window.
- **Shadows:** a cast within 4 m of one brings it (after 0.5-1.5 s
  instead of the wait).
- **Walking off** is more than 2.5 m from where you cast.
- **The catch file:** a damaged one is copied to `.bad` and the log
  starts empty, so a catch never writes over what could still be
  recovered; writes happen on a
  worker thread (to a `.tmp`, renamed over), waited for when the world
  closes.

Rejected:
- **Aiming by a ray to the water** (where the crosshair meets it): it
  fights the charge for the distance, since looking decides one and
  the meter the other.
- **Shadows only while the rod is held:** they make the shallows feel
  alive, so they wander whatever you hold, at the cost of one ray every
  0.5 s to find a spot.

## Built

`world/fishing/`: `fishing.gd` (the state machine, input, the rod,
bobber and line), `fish_species.gd` (the species table and the pick),
`catch_log.gd` (the catches in `user://catches.json`), `fishing_hud.gd`
(the meter, the line under it and the catch card, drawn in code) and
`fish_shadow.gd`. `Ocean.height_at` works out the shader's waves in
GDScript so the bobber rides them. A new input action, `use` (left
click), in `project.godot`; `world.gd` makes the `Fishing` node. The
suites point `CatchLog.file` at `user://test-catches.json`
(`tests/error_watch.gd`), so a test never touches the player's catches.
The placeholder look is recorded in `assets/CREDITS.md`.

## Bugs found in testing

- **`_fishing` left the player turned away**, which broke the next
  check (E on the orchestrator, which needs the crosshair on it). The
  test now restores the player's facing when it's done.
- **The shadows check was flaky:** it checked a shadow's depth where it
  is, but shadows wander up to 2 m from where they came, sometimes over
  deeper water. It now checks the depth at the shadow's home.

## Round 2, after the live check

The user's live check (2026-10-27, Windows): "It works fine as a
placeholder I suppose, though it will need a whole lot more work
later." What they asked for:
- The windows felt too tight. Asked whether the nibbles too: no, "you
  wait for the bite, nibbles are fine as is". So only the bite's window
  grew, 0.9 s to 1.5 s.
- A clearer indicator of when to start reeling, i.e. the bite: a
  deeper plunge (0.45 m), a splash ring and a gold "!" under the
  crosshair.
- Moving a bit while the line is out.
- The rod out of place at a wide field of view.
- Casting onto land, even with no effect.
- "Most importantly the bobber should be an object": a ball on the
  string that rolls on land as you reel it in, "but not many
  collisions to make reeling not annoying". Then: "minimal collisions
  on land, it's not the focus of fishing".
- Holding the click to reel in slowly. Their idea for the fight builds
  on it (fish pull, you move the mouse against the pull while holding);
  the fight is the next round.

My picks:
- **The bobber is a `RigidBody3D`** (a sphere 7 cm in radius, 50 g): rolling and
  bouncing come with the physics. While it rests in the water it's
  frozen and placed at the waves' height each frame, as round 1's was,
  so the nibbles' small dips read exactly and fish code is unchanged;
  it's the physics again once reeled or dragged.
- **The line as a length:** further than it, the bobber is pulled back
  (the speed away removed, plus a pull growing with the overshoot). It
  pays out as the bobber flies and as you walk, up to 30 m; holding
  shortens it 4 m/s. Snagged (taut, moving under 5 cm in 0.5 s), it
  hops.
- **The throw:** 5.2-18 m/s, 15° above the look, worked out for about
  4 m and 22 m at eye level over flat water. In the test a full charge
  aimed a little up threw 24 m.
- **Minimal collisions:** the ground's bodies (islands, bridges, the
  sea floor's chunks, the platform) are also put on a new layer 3,
  "ground", and the bobber collides with that one only. My first plan
  moved props and agents to a layer of their own instead, but checking
  the rays first showed the crosshair's line-of-sight check (C11) would
  then see through trees and rocks; the extra layer changes nothing
  else.
- **The rod:** its grip and size are scaled by the field of view's
  tangent against the 80° it was placed at, so its place on screen is
  the same from 60° to 110° (the test measures it).

Bug found in testing: the splash showed one frame late, as it was
placed before the frame's state change; it's now placed after.

## Tested

The world test's `_fish_species`, `_catch_log` and `_fishing` (with
real clicks; NOTES → D01 lists the checks). Round 1: every suite passed
on the user's Windows machine. On Linux (a cloud session, after merging
main) every Godot suite passed; the world test failed once first on S08's
flag height check (a random island seed; not D01's, passed on a
re-run). The sidecar's tests, unchanged here, failed 3 of 356: two
login-shell checks because the container's first process doesn't
reap killed children, and one program-path test that writes its fake
`claude` without an execute bit, so it fails on any Linux or macOS
(from S02, 2026-10-26; CI has been paused since). Both fixed on
`main` the same day (dev log 2026-10-27-s08-flag-check-flaky and the
S02 program-path entry) and merged in here. Round 2 (Linux, cloud
session): every Godot suite passed, one at a time (both sidecar_client
runs, session_store, world plain and fake, clusters, bridges,
performance, markdown 74/0). The look, the feel, rolling, snags and the
timings are for the live check.
