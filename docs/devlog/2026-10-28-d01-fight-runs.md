# D01: the fight's runs, arrow and view

Features: D01

A rework of the fight on branch `d01-fight`, after the user's live check
of round 3 (dev log 2026-10-27-d01-fight). The design is NOTES → D01 →
The fight.

## What the live check found (the user's, 2026-10-28)

- "The fish should pull in a direction and pull the camera towards that
  direction", with an arrow on the opposite side "so you know where to
  pull yourself".
- "There is quite literally no difference between moving the rod
  against the fish and leaving it static." In round 3 the rod only
  changed the strain while reeling and how fast the fish tired, and
  neither could be seen; the fish's path never changed.
- With reeling optional, "there should probably be a reason to give
  slack".

## What changed

- **The view turns with the rod's tip** (25° at either end): the fish
  dragging the rod pulls the view its way, and pushing the mouse back
  brings it back. The drift after the fish now leaves out that turn, so
  it doesn't undo the push.
- **An arrow beside the crosshair** points where to push, fading as
  the rod is held there (`FishFight.hint()`, drawn by `FishingHud`).
  The user's call reverses round 3's "no HUD" for the push only; the
  strain is still read from the rod, the line and the shake.
- **The rod counts.** Held against the fish it now also turns its head
  round toward you (`STEER`), ending a run; on the fish's side the reel
  hardly gains (`WITH_SLOW`). The turned heading is kept after a run
  until the fish's next turn: at first the run's end picked a fresh
  heading at once, which threw the steering away, and the bot that
  never touched the rod did almost as well.
- **Runs.** Now and then the fish runs for 1-2 s at twice its pull,
  hard to a side, with a splash and the rod jerked; between runs it
  pulls at 0.6. Reeling into a run snaps the line; let go, the drag
  gives line, taut and never at the limit, and the run tires it three
  times as fast. The splash now comes only with a run, not every turn.
- **Walking away while reeling** strains the line by how fast you go
  (`WALK_STRAIN`), the open question from round 3; my recommendation,
  chosen by the user.
- The open sea fish pulls harder (1.2, was 1.05) and has more stamina
  (26 s, was 22 s), to keep its fights in their 20-35 s.

## Giving slack, or giving line

The user asked whether letting the fish run is a good moment to give
slack ("the fish fights back and you let it?"). It's what anglers do: a
running fish is let take line against the reel's drag, and reeling
against it is the classic way to break a line. The run is where the fish
spends its strength. So letting go on a run keeps the line taut on the
drag (never slack, so the hook doesn't slip) and tires the fish fast;
slack only comes when the fish swims at you. Rejected: jumps you must
bow to (give slack on), a second signal to learn.

## Tuning

The numbers were tuned with a Python port of `FishFight` and the
test's bots (40 fights a case, several seeds), since this session's
container had no Godot. At 10 m, 20 m and 25 m, played well (let go on
runs, push where the arrow points): shallows about 7 s, drop-off about
14 s, open sea about 21 s, all landed. Timing the runs right but
leaving the rod be: shallows about 12 s, drop-off about 24 s, open sea
about a third landed, in about 40 s. Only holding the click snaps every
drop-off and open sea fish. The world test's `_fish_fight` checks these
in Godot, with a "still" bot added for the rod.
