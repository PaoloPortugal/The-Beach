# D01: the fight

Features: D01, A05, W03

The third round of fishing, on branch `d01-fight`: a click on the bite
now hooks the fish, and you fight it in, holding the click to reel and
moving the mouse against its pull. The fight is read from the world,
never from a bar. The design is NOTES → D01 → The fight.

## The brief (the user's, 2026-10-27, after round 2's live check)

A fight of our own, not a catch bar (Stardew Valley's and Webfishing's
were rejected earlier as too unoriginal). The user's idea: once hooked,
keep the click held to reel the fish in slowly; the fish pulls one way
and another; you move the mouse against its pull while holding; read
it from the world (the line's angle and strain, the rod bending).

## The choices (offered with my recommendations; the user took each)

- **What the mouse does.** Recommended, chosen: it moves the rod's tip
  while the view holds (drifting after the fish when it swims far to a
  side), the fish dragging the tip to its side. Rejected: the mouse
  keeps turning the view, and the rod points where you look. It needs
  nothing new to learn, but leaning against the fish means looking away
  from it.
- **How a fish is lost.** Recommended, chosen: two ways. The line snaps
  after about 0.7 s at its limit, and the hook slips off after about
  2 s slack. So neither holding the click nor letting go is always
  safe. Rejected: snapping only, which makes letting go always safe.
- **Length and species.** Recommended, chosen: shallows 5-10 s,
  drop-off 12-20 s, open sea 20-35 s, so most fights are easy to drop
  when an agent needs you. Species differ by pull, stamina and temper,
  and a bigger fish pulls harder. Rejected: everything under ~15 s.
- **A05 during a fight.** Recommended, chosen: the corner count pulses
  once on a new wait, so the old decision holds without the bar.
  Rejected: nothing new.
- **No colour on the line** (mine): it would be a bar by another name.

## My picks

- **A model of its own**, `FishFight`, on the water's plane: Fishing
  steps it and shows it. The world test can then play whole fights in
  a loop without the world.
- **Tuned with a bot.** A scratch simulation played 40 fights per water
  three ways: "good" (pushes the mouse against the fish's side, lets go
  above a strain of 0.85, reels again below 0.7), "hold" (only reels)
  and "idle" (never reels). The first numbers gave open sea fights of
  ~19 s. More stamina barely helped: the length came from the reel
  speed. So a running fish now slows the reel more (by 0.8 × its away
  pull instead of 0.6). The result: shallows ~6 s, drop-off ~15 s, open
  sea ~22 s for the bot. A person will be slower, which is about the
  target. "Hold" lands every shallows fish but snaps most drop-off and
  nearly all open sea fish. "Idle" loses them all. The world test keeps
  a 12-fight version of this.
- **Straight runs and side runs ask for different things.** On a
  straight run, letting go is the answer (the reel gives line). On a
  side run, the answer is the mouse against it. A fish turns toward you
  on 15 % of its turns, and then the slack is the danger.
- **The rod bends** in four pieces (6°, 12°, 18° at the limit), a
  placeholder like the rest. **The view shakes** through the camera's
  `h_offset`/`v_offset`, which nothing else uses.
- **The view is held** by a new `Player.look_held` rather than turning
  the controls off, so walking still works mid-fight.
- **A fish never swims onto land:** it turns instead (a depth ray each
  frame).
